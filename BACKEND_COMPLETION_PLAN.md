# Completing the Clothing Star discovery backend

Decision and implementation order, checked **21 November 2026**. The first release covers Bonkers Corner, Burger Bae, Everdion, Genrage, Hauntrex, JNORSS, Powerlook, Sagacity and WTFlex. Snitch remains outside the release. A shopper can discover and compare styles here and complete a purchase at the merchant.

**Start with the catalogue already collected.** Prove which products each department and garment section contains, give each variant one reviewed source for its offer, then build text and photo search over the same inventory. The first vertical slice is a Bonkers shopper looking for a black hoodie in M under ₹1,500. JNORSS tests what happens when colour or audience is missing, and WTFlex tests denim and multi-part sizes. Every run reports coverage for all nine brands from day one.

## 3. Make a search feel like a marketplace

**One query planner for text, structured filters and photos.** Parse department, garment, size system, garment colour, fit, material, budget bounds, price basis, brand and availability into explicit constraints. Retain the other words as design or name text. A shopper can search across departments or select one. Explicit unisex items can qualify for a men's or women's search. Unknown department stays available in unfiltered browsing.

| Step | Behaviour to implement |
| --- | --- |
| Find eligible variants | Match *one merchant variant* to all hard requirements. Include product-level facts only when they apply to that variant. A cheaper XS cannot qualify an M. Preserve independent top and bottom choices in sets. Accept “under ₹1,500” as a strict upper bound and “up to ₹1,500” as inclusive. Expose item price or delivered total explicitly. Unknown delivery cost gets its own group. |
| Retrieve candidates | Build a weighted Postgres text index from product name and SKU aliases, reviewed garment/fit/design evidence, source type/tags, verified collection labels and product-specific descriptions. Give exact SKU its own indexed lookup that returns its actual variant. Retain original labels and a short, attributable reason for a match. Exclude sitewide banners, cross-sell copy and generic promotional tags from product evidence. |
| Understand ordinary wording | Support scoped alias and typo suggestions, such as “hoodi” → hoodie, and bounded design relations such as thunder → lightning. Treat cotton as material and “no polyester” as a composition constraint requiring sufficient evidence. Support alternatives within a facet, e.g. black **or** grey. Preserve unrecognised design terms for lexical search. Never fuzzy-correct a size, product code or price. |
| Rank the first results | First require the correct result group and eligible variant. In a relevance sort, rank exact product/SKU or named design first, followed by strong field-specific evidence, then descriptive matches. Show related designs in a labelled group when the exact design is absent. Use freshness and evidence strength within the group and favourites as a tie-break. Apply diversity to otherwise comparable styles. Price sort uses the qualifying variant. Commission never buys a higher organic rank. |
| Return results, counts and explanations | Count distinct styles and compute facet counts over the same eligible variants. For selectable alternatives within a facet, recompute that facet with the other active constraints held fixed. Deduplicate galleries and sizes, then paginate. Return matched product/variant IDs, price basis, stock/fulfilment state, checked times, reasons, result group, catalogue revision and cursor. Search, photo search, brand browsing and comparison consume this contract. |
| Handle empty or stale results | Offer one explicit relaxation at a time with its count. For a black thunder hoodie, a lightning-design hoodie with unknown garment colour and sold-out sizes belongs in last-seen browsing only when the shopper chooses the corresponding relaxation. Surface helpful alternatives such as “all colours” or “include sweatshirts” with a clear change in scope. |

Start with a stored weighted `tsvector` and GIN index, and use trigram matching against a bounded term/name dictionary for typos. The database reports `pg_trgm` available but uninstalled. Enable and measure it only in its implementation step. Inspect real query plans and compare against the current title-plus-description baseline. A later semantic-text experiment must earn its place in held-out relevance tests. [Postgres text ranking](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/textsearch-controls.html) · [Text indexes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/textsearch-indexes.html) · [Trigram matching](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/pgtrgm.html)

**Example acceptance case.** For “black oversized hoodie with a racing print, M, under ₹1,500”, prove hoodie, oversized, garment colour, M, price and the requested stock status on a compatible item/variant. Source wording about the actual design must support racing. “Racing fit”, a racing collection's promotional banner, or the mere presence of a car cannot establish a racing print. A qualifying description-only match should beat a title match on an ineligible item. If no eligible result exists, give the reason and the useful nearest alternative.

## 4. Add photo search without a paid inference service

**Recommended first implementation.** Once there are images cleared for embedding, encode one approved representative view per style or colourway on an existing computer with the quantized Marqo FashionCLIP model and ONNX Runtime CPU. If permission and coverage allow, add consistent front/back comparisons so graphic prints can match the right angle. The shopper selects or crops one garment from a photo. Encode it once, apply the same hard SQL filters as text search, score the eligible gallery vectors by cosine similarity and group the best relevant views by style. A cheaper variant in a different colourway cannot inherit a visual match. Show visual similarity and matched text evidence separately. [FashionCLIP model card](https://huggingface.co/Marqo/marqo-fashionCLIP) · [ONNX Runtime](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/get-started/with-python.html)

**What actually ran on 21 November.** The pinned 87,301,119-byte model ran locally with ONNX Runtime 1.20.0 on CPU, two inference threads. The gallery contained 84 distinct public Fashion-MNIST clothing/accessory images, 12 in each of seven classes. Each class supplied five different held-out queries. Exact cosine search returned the right class first for **24/35** queries, compared with **21/35** for raw grayscale pixels on the same split. Pullover scored 3/5, shirt 2/5 and coat 1/5. Warm median and p95 for encoding one query and searching this gallery were **95 ms and 104 ms** in the measured run. This demonstrates fee-free local execution. The small grayscale test establishes coarse category retrieval only. Brand inventory, graphics, colour fidelity, phone photos and full-scale latency still need testing. [Model files at the tested revision](https://huggingface.co/Marqo/marqo-fashionCLIP/tree/44f4c655124ed71e90cbf528d82f508d69d8a81b/onnx) · [Original Fashion-MNIST dataset](https://github.com/zalandoresearch/fashion-mnist)

To reproduce, put `vision_model_quantized.onnx` from the linked revision and the original `t10k-images-idx3-ubyte.gz` and `t10k-labels-idx1-ubyte.gz` from the linked data revision in one local folder. Run the command below with Python, NumPy, Pillow and ONNX Runtime. The script verifies all three SHA-256 hashes, separates gallery/query pixels, and reports per-class hits and warm timings. It takes file paths and makes no outbound calls. [Benchmark script](scripts/benchmark-photo-search.py) · [Pinned dataset files](https://github.com/zalandoresearch/fashion-mnist/tree/b2617bb6d3ffa2e429640350f613e3291e10b141/data/fashion)

```powershell
python scripts/benchmark-photo-search.py data/backend-plan-20261121
```

- **Cost and scale.** A 512-dimensional float32 vector uses 2,048 bytes. One per 8,325 styles is about 16.3 MiB. One per 56,302 gallery references is about 110 MiB before indexes, metadata and backups. Supabase is already about 185 MiB against the project's 250 MB pilot target. Begin with an exact local array of approved vectors plus IDs, rebuildable from a private manifest. Postgres owns eligibility and index version. Benchmark full-catalogue and highly selective filters before considering pgvector. It is available but uninstalled. Local inference still needs electricity, bandwidth and an available machine. Measure deployment uptime, concurrent requests, memory and egress before promising a free public service. [pgvector exact and approximate search](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector)
- **Permissions and privacy.** Track approval to fetch/cache, compute embeddings and publicly display an image independently. Choose merchant-permitted or owned/licensed assets for the fashion pilot. A thumbnail URL alone does not establish those uses. Process query photos in memory, discard EXIF and discard the photo and query vector after the request. Bound upload size, decoded pixels, content types, concurrency and timeouts. Keep photos and query vectors out of logs, Redis and ImageKit. Remove a revoked catalogue asset and its vector from the index before serving the next revision.
- **Quality before rollout.** Use at least 60 consented or licensed real-world queries, with separate gallery images for the relevant style. Include outfit crops, hanger vs model shots, back prints, colourways, confusing near-neighbours and photos with no reasonable match. Hold out styles/near-duplicate photos together. Compare a simple appearance baseline and the encoder on identical eligible inventory. Measure exact-style recall@5 where the style exists, human-rated lookalike relevance in the first five, false positives on no-match photos, and warm/cold latency. Ship photo matching only for the evaluated scope. If nothing is cleared or indexing is unavailable, keep text search usable and show the actual visual coverage.
- **A photo plus a request.** For “like this, black, waist 28, under ₹2,000”, first resolve the qualifying variants. Score the photo against just their applicable views. If the shopper also types a design preference, test combining the text and visual rank lists with reciprocal rank fusion against either list alone. Keep exact named items and documented requirements in their proper groups. Calibrate any no-match threshold on held-out examples with different backgrounds and lighting. A cosine score is unsuitable as a shopper-facing percentage of certainty.
